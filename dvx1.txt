Just found the drive with the footage back-up. Opened it up with the Sony software to check the metadata and play it. We shot UHD, 25P and 1/50 shutter. The sit-downs were all inside, natural backgrounds. I looked at the first one that was in a house with the kitchen as the background with 60hz tungsten practicals in the background and I didn't see any flickering on my laptop and we never heard anything from the client about issues with flicker, either. The second interview was in an office, but I believe we turned all existing lights out and used our LED's and augmented the daylight coming in. I also didn't see any flicker in the b-roll in the office after the interview. There was a lot of daylight coming in lighting the room up. I can see the light from tungsten cans in the ceiling reflecting on the leather seats, but I don't recall if there were fluorescent lights in there and there were no(inadvertent) shots of the ceiling to tell if they had flo's in there.

EDIT:

 I did have a wide shot of the room, no flo's and there was a big monitor on and no flicker in it, either.

Bern - reading your post just reminded me of a shoot I did about 3 months ago. Simple conf room recording of an important presenter for a corporation here in the states. So - just me, Fs7 and wireless mics. No lights wanted - just there to record for employees to watch later.

 I spent the first 20 minutes after arriving trying to find a setting in camera that didn't cause the flickering from the overheads in the room. No frame rate or shutter speed (including off) would totally get rid of it (some were better than others at minimizing). I had never run into this before in 25+ years of shooting. They were the type of lights - I usually associate with flourescents - that have the bulbs facing up (unseen) for bounced light downward. So perhaps this is my first encounter with that type of LED.
